# Visual Language

## Composition Rules
- Frame for emotion, not fullness.
- Use asymmetry and negative space to create tension.
- Let the eye move from quiet to anchor.

## Negative Space
- Negative space is a design asset.
- It creates scale and allows discovery.
- Reserve voids around the black hole and key depth planes.

## Color Palette
- Primary: Deep black, midnight blue.
- Secondary: Soft gray, muted cyan.
- Accent: Warm amber, muted gold.
- Avoid bright or saturated hues.

## Lighting
- Light is implied, not explicit.
- Keep highlights soft and directional.
- Use darkness to shape the scene.

## Scale
- Build depth through layered planes.
- Large emptiness makes objects meaningful.
- Small details add richness without clutter.

## Depth
- Background, nebula, dust, stars, and black hole must occupy distinct planes.
- Ensure each layer reads at a different distance.
- Avoid collapsing space into a flat visual field.

## Focal Points
- The black hole is the emotional anchor.
- Secondary focal flow moves from stars to dust to the anchor.
- Keep other elements supporting, not competing.

## Hero Object Philosophy
- Hero objects are quiet and purposeful.
- They should feel earned through composition.
- The black hole is hero object one; everything else supports it.

## Black Hole Composition
- The black hole should be subtle and slightly offset.
- It should be distant, mysterious, and only gradually revealed.
- Use the accretion disk as a warm, muted guide.

## Earth Composition
- Earth is not part of the current opening sequence.
- If introduced later, it should be treated as a secondary discovery, not a primary anchor.
